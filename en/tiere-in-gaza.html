Animals in Gaza

Where people are starving and hospitals are missing, animals have hardly a chance.

Hunger

Many animals starve or are severely emaciated. In the worst phases of hunger, helpers in Gaza shared their own rice, pasta and tuna with the animals.1 Many owners could no longer feed their animals and had to set them free.2

Hardly any vets

In June 2026, only two veterinary clinics for pets were still operating, with a severe shortage of medicines, vaccines and supplies. The Israeli agency COGAT says it approves the import of animal vaccines; local vets say many supplies are nevertheless not available.3 Vets sometimes treat with expired medicines or with drugs meant for humans.1

Rabies

According to a doctor in Gaza, in May 2026 there was neither rabies vaccine nor rabies serum in the Gaza Strip.4 Every bite from a sick animal can therefore become life-threatening – for animals and people. Our project is in an area where our team has to reckon with rabies.

Tetanus

Bites, scratches and injuries from rubble, metal and debris are a typical route for tetanus – for the animals and for the people who rescue them. Treating bite wounds therefore also includes a tetanus vaccination.4

Parvovirus & distemper

Vets in Gaza treat dogs and cats with parvovirus, malnutrition, anaemia and infections.1 Where animals live unvaccinated, weakened and in close quarters, such diseases – like distemper – spread especially fast. That is why our quarantine area is so important.

More and more street animals

Without neutering, more and more animals are born on the street – into hunger, disease and danger. Neutering projects for dogs and cats are therefore one of our next steps.

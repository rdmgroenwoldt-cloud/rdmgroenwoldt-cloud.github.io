A closer look

The issues in detail

Tap to expand.

Rubbish and rubbleIllegal dumps between the tents
  • Since October 2023, the municipal services have no longer been able to reach the main landfill, Juhor ad-Dik; the area is under Israeli control. As a result, a huge makeshift dump grew in the middle of Gaza City at the Firas market (Reuters, 12 Feb 2026).
  • According to UNDP, more than 140 illegal rubbish dumps have appeared near residential areas and camps.
  • In 80% of the camps for displaced people, rats and other disease carriers are frequently observed (UNICEF, 29 May 2026).
  • According to the UN Environment Programme UNEP, around 61 million tonnes of rubble have been created; about 15% of it could be contaminated with asbestos, industrial waste or heavy metals (model calculation, 23 Sep 2025). Unexploded ordnance also lies in the debris (WHO, 2 Oct 2026).
No sewage system, hardly any clean waterAlmost 90% of facilities destroyed or damaged
  • According to Doctors Without Borders, almost 90% of water and sanitation facilities are destroyed or damaged (28 Apr 2026).
  • According to UNEP, no sewage treatment plant is in operation; cesspits and broken pipes pollute groundwater and the coast.
  • On average, 4.5 to 6 litres of water are available per person per day. By comparison: in Europe it is about 145 litres (UNICEF, 29 May 2026).
  • During the heavy rain in November 2026, rainwater mixed with sewage; suction trucks pumped the mixture out of 58 emergency shelters (OCHA).
Epidemics and diseasesScabies, meningitis, hepatitis, polio
  • Scabies and skin diseases: 52% of households are affected (OCHA, 13 Nov 2026). In clinics run by Doctors Without Borders, respiratory infections (42%), skin diseases such as scabies and lice (17%) and diarrhoea (8%) were the most common reasons for a visit.
  • Meningitis: 413 suspected cases in five weeks, mainly among infants (OCHA, 13 Nov 2026).
  • Hepatitis A / jaundice: By March 2024, the WHO had already counted over 19,000 cases of acute jaundice. Current overall figures are not being published at the moment; the UN stepped up monitoring in November 2026.
  • Polio: In August 2024, the WHO confirmed the first polio case in Gaza in 25 years – a ten-month-old unvaccinated child is paralysed. Vaccination campaigns for hundreds of thousands of children have been running since then.
  • Guillain-Barré syndrome (a form of nerve paralysis): according to the WHO, 153 cases and 22 deaths; the most important medicine was out of stock (January 2026).
Winter and rainy seasonFlooded tents, cold, deaths
  • Around 65% of people – about 1.4 million – live in tents or makeshift shelters (OCHA, 8 Nov 2026).
  • In November 2026, an infant drowned in a camp; a sliding sand mound buried tents and killed five people, including three children (OCHA).
  • In the winter of 2025/26, at least ten children died of hypothermia, according to UNICEF (26 Jan 2026).
  • 121,000 tents and 561,000 tarpaulins are needed; at the beginning of November, only about 2,800 tents were in stock in Gaza. Over 150,000 tents are stored outside the Gaza Strip (OCHA).
Sick people without treatmentCancer, dialysis, diabetes – and waiting to leave
  • Cancer: Around 11,000 cancer patients have no specialist treatment or diagnostics (UN Palestine, Feb 2026). 55% of chemotherapy drugs are missing; there are only four pathologists for tissue examinations (OCHA, Oct 2026).
  • Dialysis: Over 1,100 people depend on dialysis; a substance needed for it (citrate) is critically scarce (WHO, OCHA).
  • Diabetes: Insulin for type 1 diabetes is scarce; according to Doctors Without Borders, medicines for high blood pressure and heart disease are also missing.
  • Leaving for treatment: Over 18,500 people are waiting. According to the WHO, at least 1,092 of them died while waiting (July 2024 to November 2025) – in the WHO's assessment, the figure is probably too low. In the first week of November 2026, 119 patients were able to leave.
Too few aid deliveriesPromised: 600 trucks per day
  • With the ceasefire in October 2025, 600 trucks per day were promised. According to UN figures, an average of around 113 UN trucks per day arrived up to December 2025 (AP analysis).
  • At the beginning of November 2026, the UN and partners unloaded only 228 trucks in a week. Since 1 November 2026, the route via Egypt has been largely suspended (OCHA).
  • Engine oil, spare parts, generators and construction timber are especially strictly restricted; goods with possible military use (“dual use”) are considered the main obstacle. There is no complete public list of these goods.
  • Israel's view: The responsible Israeli authority COGAT states that more than 65,000 trucks have entered Gaza since the ceasefire and that aid exceeds the need; it says the UN counts only its own trucks. UN and COGAT figures are therefore not directly comparable.
Missing medicines and baby foodAnaesthetics and painkillers, infant formula, prosthetics
  • According to the WHO, in March 2026 46% of key medicines and 66% of consumables were out of stock; anaesthetics were scarce.
  • In November 2026, fever reducers, antibiotics and remedies for diarrhoea and vomiting were increasingly missing (OCHA).
  • In November 2025, UNICEF reported that 938,000 bottles of ready-to-feed infant formula were not allowed in. COGAT disputed this in part.
  • According to the WHO, of 2,277 people with amputations who were assessed, only 502 received prosthetics; deliveries of assistive devices waited 136 days on average.
  • According to the WHO, most applications for medicines are now approved (almost 87%, October 2026) – shortages still occur because only one border crossing is open.
What Doctors Without Borders reportsReports from 2025 and 2026
  • “This is not a ceasefire” (10 Apr 2026): According to MSF, all of the organisation's relief supplies have been blocked since 1 January 2026; MSF and 36 other aid organisations had their registration withdrawn.
  • “Water as a weapon” (28 Apr 2026): About one third of MSF's applications for water and sanitation supplies were rejected or never answered.
  • Report of 5 Oct 2026: According to MSF, engine oil and spare parts are missing; even scabies cream is said to be restricted. Most people have less than 6 litres of water a day.
  • Distribution sites (August 2025): In two MSF clinics, 1,380 injured people and 28 dead arrived from the GHF food distributions within seven weeks; the GHF disputes this account.
  • The assessments in the reports are those of Doctors Without Borders.

What we do

How the network helps

The projects in the network help where the situation is hardest – and Dar Al-Rahma supports them.

Medical careMedical Point North Gaza

The Medical Point in Beit Lahia cares for the injured, treats burns, wounds and chronically ill people, and hands out medicines. We help with supplies, medicines and donations.

Basic supplies and winter aidFood, water, blankets, tarpaulins

Food, drinking water, hygiene items, blankets and tarpaulins for families who have lost everything – especially for children, older people and people with disabilities. This area is still being set up.

Water & familiesHope4Humanity · Gazahilfspool

Hope4Humanity has distributed 15,000 litres of drinking water in half a year and is planning two wells. The Gazahilfspool supplies 12 families with food and medicines through regular sponsorships – and most recently brought baby milk for critically ill children at the Al-Rantisi Children's Hospital.

ChildrenKids4Hope

Kids4Hope gives 140 orphaned children lessons, structure and care – a touch of normality in the midst of hardship.

Health for people and animalsPreventing epidemics and rabies

Many diseases jump between animals and people. That is why the network also supports Cat Rescue and rabies prevention.
